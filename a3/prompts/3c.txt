Imagine mailing a box of crackers with nothing around them. They'd get smashed! The extra air in a chip bag works like a pillow, protecting the chips while trucks bump them around. It's usually nitrogen, which also keeps chips from going stale. Cereal and pasta settle, too: the box is filled by weight at the factory, then the pieces shake down and pack tighter on the way to the store. Machines also fill packages fast, so they need a little wiggle room. Sometimes, though, a bigger box just looks better on the shelf.
